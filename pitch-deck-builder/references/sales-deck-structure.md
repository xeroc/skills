# The 6-Act Sales Deck Structure

## Why This Structure Works

Founders pitch products. Investors buy stories.

The 6-act structure maps to how humans make decisions under uncertainty:

1. **Notice something** (market forces create urgency)
2. **Feel the pain** (vignettes create empathy)
3. **Imagine the alternative** (vision creates desire)
4. **See the path** (solution creates confidence)
5. **Trust the guide** (proof creates belief)
6. **Take the step** (ask creates action)

Skip any act and the story breaks. Jump to the solution without establishing pain? You sound like every other startup. Show proof without establishing the market? The numbers have no context.

## Act-by-Act Breakdown

### Act 1: Market Forces (Why Now)

**Purpose**: Create urgency. Make the investor feel this is inevitable.

**What goes here**:

- Macro trend with real numbers (cite sources)
- Why this moment, not last year or next year
- Catalyst or validation signal (someone credible confirmed the thesis)

**What doesn't go here**:

- Your product
- Your features
- Projections

**Example** (Tributary):

> $316B in stablecoins. Solana holds $15.2B. The Solana Foundation shipped their own delegation primitive — confirming automated money movement is core infrastructure. The infrastructure layer exists. The automation layer doesn't.

**Common mistake**: Dumping every market stat. Pick one number that hits. ">$316B stablecoin market cap" is stronger than a table of five metrics.

### Act 2: Their Pain (Current Challenges)

**Purpose**: Create empathy. Make the investor feel the pain.

**What goes here**:

- 2-3 vivid vignettes from different segments
- All pointing to the same root cause
- Real desires, not "lack of solution"

**What doesn't go here**:

- Your product
- How big the market is (that's Act 1)
- Abstract statements ("there is inefficiency")

**Vignette formula**: [Specific person/org] + [specific situation] + [specific consequence of the problem].

**Example** (Tributary):

> A DAO with $50M treasury. Market moves at 2am. Someone needs to wake up, check prices, sign a rebalancing transaction. Every time. Or the portfolio drifts.

**Common mistake**: Saying "there's no platform that does X." That's a lack of solution. Instead: "A DAO treasurer sets a 3am alarm to rebalance." That's a real desire with real pain.

### Act 3: Future Vision (The Dream)

**Purpose**: Create desire. Make the investor want to live in this future.

**What goes here**:

- "What if" framing for each vignette from Act 2
- Same segments, now unblocked
- One shared mechanism that resolves all of them
- Non-custodial / trustless / safe language if applicable

**What doesn't go here**:

- Your product name
- Technical details
- Implementation specifics

**Example** (Tributary):

> What if a DAO could rebalance automatically — only when the oracle confirms a 5% drift, only within approved parameters, non-custodial the whole time?

**Common mistake**: Revealing the product too early. Act 3 is about the dream, not the bridge. The investor should be thinking "I want that world" before they learn how you build it.

### Act 4: The Bridge (Your Solution)

**Purpose**: Create confidence. Show how the dream becomes real.

**What goes here**:

- Your product name and one-line description
- The core primitive / mechanism (one diagram)
- Walk through with a single example
- Show composability / flexibility by swapping parts
- Fold in competitive differentiation as "why no one else does this"

**What doesn't go here**:

- Every feature you've built
- Competitive comparison tables (unless brief and folded in)
- Implementation details (those go in speaker notes)

**Example** (Tributary):

> WHEN (condition) → PULL (amount) → ROUTE (to any on-chain program)
> Walk through: "WHEN weekly → PULL $100 → ROUTE swap to SOL."
> Then: "Replace 'weekly' with 'when price drops 5%' and 'swap' with 'stake.' Same primitive. Different product."

**Common mistake**: Listing features. Instead, show one example, then show how swapping parts creates new products. That's the composability story.

### Act 5: Proof Points (Why Believe)

**Purpose**: Create trust. Make the investor believe you can deliver.

**What goes here**:

- Traction metrics (big numbers first)
- Live integrations / customers
- Team credentials (condensed)
- Ecosystem enablement (if platform play — business categories as grid)

**What doesn't go here**:

- Projections
- "If we capture 1% of..."
- Vague claims without evidence

**Example** (Tributary):

> 4,000+ payments. $12,000+ transferred. 9 production systems. Ottersec verified. 6+ integrations. One founder. Zero funding.

**Common mistake**: Burying the lead. Put the strongest metric first. "4,000 payments, zero marketing" hits harder than "we have a robust SDK."

### Act 6: Next Steps (The Path)

**Purpose**: Create action. Make the investor know exactly what you need.

**What goes here**:

- The ask (amount, stage)
- Allocation table (where the money goes)
- 12-month arc with verifiable milestones
- Close with the one-liner

**What doesn't go here**:

- Apologies for the amount
- Vague "we're flexible"
- Detailed financial models

**Example** (Tributary):

> <$250K pre-seed. 30% audit. 27% composable layer. 27% growth. 16% ops. Month 3: audit. Month 6: composable live. Month 9: 15+ integrations. Month 12: seed raise on real metrics.

**Common mistake**: Saying "we're flexible on amount." It reads as unsure. State a number or range. "We're raising $200-250K" is confident.

## The Flip Test

After drafting, read only the title sentences in sequence. They must tell a complete story without any slide body text.

**Passing flip test** (Tributary):

1. $316B in stablecoins. Zero automation.
2. Money that can't act on its own is money that can't scale.
3. What if money could act within boundaries you set?
4. Tributary: the composable automation layer for self-driving money.
5. Built. Live. The market is already building on it.
6. Self-driving money starts now.

**Failing flip test** (generic):

1. Market Opportunity
2. The Problem
3. Our Vision
4. The Solution
5. Traction
6. The Ask

Generic labels mean the slides don't tell a story.

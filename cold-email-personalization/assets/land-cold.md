# Cold Outreach Skill Resource

## Quick Reference Card

**Core Principle:** _Make one real person feel like you wrote it only for them._

**The Rule:** If they believe you picked them on purpose, they read. If they sense a blast, they're gone before line two.

---

## Platform-Specific Mechanics

### LinkedIn

- **Connection Request:** Send with NO note. Empty requests get accepted more often.
- **Timing:** Wait a few days after they accept before messaging.
- **No Reply After 2 Weeks:** Send an InMail with NO subject line (LinkedIn bolds subjects → readers see bold as "ad").
- **First Line = Notification Preview:** It must earn the open by itself.

### Email

- **Subject:** Short, lowercase, like a person not a campaign (_"quick one about [their thing]"_).
- **Length:** Keep under ~90 words.
- **First Line:** Never about you.

### X DMs

- **Format:** No subject, lowercase, 2-4 lines max.
- **Context:** Lean on existing context (follow-backs, post replies).

## The 9 Tactics

| #   | Tactic                           | Why It Works                                                                   |
| --- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | **Make them feel chosen**        | Use specificity: number, reason, list. Vague = blast.                          |
| 2   | **Open with a hook about THEM**  | Never start with "we," "I," or company name. Never open with "I noticed that." |
| 3   | **Say their objection first**    | Name why they'd ignore you to disarm it.                                       |
| 4   | **Make the ask weightless**      | Don't ask for 30 min. Ask for a glance.                                        |
| 5   | **Prove demand quietly**         | Small, specific proof beats adjectives.                                        |
| 6   | **Connect to them specifically** | "You wrote about X, here's the fix for exactly that."                          |
| 7   | **Use lowercase first name**     | "hey tom," not "hey Tom" (automations auto-capitalize).                        |
| 8   | **Keep it short**                | Under 50 words for DMs.                                                        |
| 9   | **One ask per message**          | Two asks = double the work = no reply.                                         |

## Sound Like a Human, Not a System

### 🚨 AI Tell-Tale Signs (FIX THESE)

- Tidy wordplay and balanced phrases
- Lists of three, everywhere
- Neat summary lines with a bow
- Every sentence same length and shape
- Generic enthusiasm ("amazing opportunity," "genuinely excited")
- Em-dashes and "I hope this finds you well"

### ✅ Human Writing Looks Like

- Lowercase, contractions, occasional fragments
- Lumpy rhythm (short → long → short)
- One concrete detail instead of smooth abstraction
- Small aside no template would bother with ("bit of a long shot")

**Rule of Thumb:** If it reads clean and balanced, rough it up. Cut a connective. Break symmetry. Let one line run short. Smooth is forgettable.

---

## What Kills a Cold Message

✗ "Figured I'd reach out" as opener  
✗ Leading with title, company, or what you built  
✗ More than one ask  
✗ Wall of text  
✗ Adjectives where proof belongs  
✗ Asking for 30 minutes up front

## Follow-Up That Works

**Instead of:** "Just bumping this up..."

**Send:**

```
hey [name],
just checking in, any thoughts on [the thing]?
wanted to give you first dibs.
no worries if it's not the right fit right now.
```

**Why It Works:** The last line hands them an easy no → they stop bracing → they actually answer.

## Templates to Adapt

### You Have No Proof Yet

```
hey [name] :)
bit of a long shot. i'm building [one line on what it does],
and you're one of maybe five people whose honest take i'd actually trust.
can i show you what i've got?
```

### Mutual Connection Sent You

```
hey [name], [mutual] said you're the person who actually gets [topic].
i'm working on [one line] and would love your eyes on it for two minutes.
open to it?
```

### They Liked/Commented on Your Post

```
hey [name], saw your comment on my post about [topic].
[one real reaction to it]. building something around exactly that.
want an early look?
```

### They Gave You a Soft No

```
totally fair, [name], i won't chase you.
want me to send over [the one genuinely useful thing]
so it's there when the timing's better?
```

## The Bigger Idea

Cold outreach isn't luck. It's craft with a handful of rules.

**The frame to keep in your head:** Every tactic above is just a different way to prove the message is for _them_, specifically. Keep that in your head and most of the rules write themselves.

# First Round "Levels of PMF" — Pitch Audit Framework

Distilled from firstround.com/levels (Session 01: Levels of PMF, 2024-08-04).
Use this framework to audit and improve existing pitch decks, especially for pre-seed/seed B2B or protocol startups.

---

## The 4 Levels of PMF

PMF is not binary — it progresses through levels. The journey is a two-way ladder (you can regress).

### Level 1: Nascent
- **Focus:** Find a problem worth solving for 3-5 customers. Build something they NEED.
- **Primary dimension:** Satisfaction
- **Hallmarks:** No repeatability. Each customer's needs are slightly different. Finding the marginal customer is hard.
- **Benchmarks:** <10 team, pre-seed/seed, 3-5 customers, $0-$500K ARR, 1/10-20 warm intros convert, don't know gross margin or burn multiple yet.
- **Trap:** Getting stuck for 6-18 months making small changes. Jack Altman (Lattice): "If you're stuck, you're probably not a 10% adjustment away — you're a 200% adjustment away."

### Level 2: Developing
- **Focus:** Demand — find scalable channels beyond warm intros.
- **Primary dimension:** Demand
- **Hallmarks:** Glimering signs the marginal customer is within reach. ~20 customers, repeatability emerging. One of two levels where most companies get stuck.
- **Benchmarks:** Up to 20 team, seed/Series A, 5-25 customers, $500K-$5M ARR, 1st-call-to-close ~10%, magic number 0.5-0.75, gross margin >50%, burn multiple <5x, NRR >100%.

### Level 3: Strong
- **Focus:** Efficiency — CAC payback, LTV/CAC, GTM refinement.
- **Primary dimension:** Efficiency
- **Hallmarks:** Demand floodgates open. Inbound, word-of-mouth. Company feels like it's humming.
- **Benchmarks:** 30-100 team, Series A/B/C, 25-100 customers, $5-25M ARR, 1st-call-to-close 10-15%, magic number >0.75, CAC payback <18mo, NRR >110%, churn <10%, gross margin 60-70%+, burn multiple 1-3x.

### Level 4: Extreme
- **Focus:** Expand TAM — multi-product, new markets, new buyers.
- **Primary dimension:** All three firing simultaneously.
- **Hallmarks:** Brand inflection. Becoming the "Kleenex" of your category. Tremendous consistency in winning target persona.
- **Benchmarks:** 100+ team, Series C+, 100+ customers, $25M+ ARR, 1st-call-to-close >15%, magic number >1, CAC payback <12mo, NRR >120%, churn <10%, gross margin 80%+, burn multiple 0-1x.

## The 3 Dimensions

- **Satisfaction:** How happy are customers? Do they NEED (not love) the product? Would they be disappointed if it disappeared?
- **Demand:** Lines out the door? How quickly and how much can you sell?
- **Efficiency:** Can the product grow repeatably? CAC, sales conversion, CAC payback, NRR, COGS, gross margin, burn multiple.

These are in tension — optimizing one often harms another. The art is knowing which to prioritize at each level.

## The 4Ps Levers (to get unstuck)

| Lever | Question | When to pull |
|-------|----------|--------------|
| **Persona** | Who would benefit most? Too narrow, too broad, or wrong? | Wrong customers, scattered feedback |
| **Problem** | Is this urgent and important? Painkiller or vitamin? | Low urgency, "nice to have" signals |
| **Promise** | Does your UVP resonate? (Most overlooked lever) | Product works but messaging doesn't land |
| **Product** | Does the product deliver on the promise? Would they pay? | Right audience, wrong solution |

Examples: Plaid pivoted persona (consumer → fintech infra). Lattice pivoted problem (OKRs → performance management). Ironclad pivoted promise (AI legal assistant → CLM platform).

## The Friend Zone Test (Rick Song, Persona)

The most important audit question for Level 1 pitches: **Do customers like but not NEED your product?**

Ask early customers directly:
- "If a competitor charged 50% less, would you switch?"
- "If we increase the price, would you push back?"
- "Is this a game changer? If it went away, how big a deal would that be?"

Yellow flags:
- Customers wouldn't be disappointed if product disappeared tomorrow
- Usage is low and not growing over 6 months
- The most important feature is different for each customer (consulting territory)
- Sales cycles too long; deals fall apart if champion leaves
- Design partners hesitant about converting to paid

## The Discovery Story Pattern

The strongest pitch opening for Level 1 companies: lead with a customer who used your product in a way you didn't design for.

**Why it works:** Investors trust user-discovered insights more than founder-declared visions. It proves the market pulled the product forward.

**Examples from First Round:**
- Plaid built a budgeting app. A friend asked to license the backend. Company pivoted.
- Ironclad watched a customer use their product on two monitors. Realized the niche was legal ops routine transactions, not AI legal assistance.

**How to structure it:**
1. "We built X for purpose Y."
2. "Then customer Z used it for purpose W — which we never considered."
3. "That revealed what we actually are: [refined product thesis]."
4. "Now we're building the version that realization demands."

This replaces generic "The Problem" slides. It proves satisfaction and reveals the persona organically.

## Pre-Seed Pitch Best Practices (derived from the framework)

### Cut
- Top-down TAM/SAM/SOM slides — red flag at pre-seed. First Round explicitly flags these.
- Hockey-stick revenue projections — directional unit economics only.
- Full ecosystem/opportunity grids (14 hypothetical use cases) — signals lack of focus. Show 2-3 concrete examples max.

### Add
- Bottom-up unit economics: "N integrators × $X/mo volume × Y% fee = $Z/mo revenue." Let investors stress-test the math.
- A single named beachhead persona. Filter all traction through it.
- "What Users Taught Us" section showing the pattern across integrations.
- Concrete Level 2 targets: "15+ integrations, $X/mo protocol revenue, 2-3 verticals proven, one use case where we're the default."

### Emphasize
- Satisfaction: organic inbound, zero marketing, integrators building real products.
- Founder-market fit: execution velocity (shipped systems, zero funding).
- Efficiency story (even at Level 1): protocol economics, near-zero marginal cost, no custody/COGS.

### De-emphasize
- Perfect financials, complex market sizing, corporate structure.
- Vanity metrics (signups, downloads) — use real usage/volume data instead.

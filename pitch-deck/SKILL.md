# Pitch Deck

Use when building pitch decks for hackathons (Colosseum, ETHGlobal, etc.), accelerator applications (YC), or investor pitches. Covers one-liner compression, blurb writing, founder video production, and slide construction. Based on methodology from Colosseum hackathon winners who mentored 100+ early-stage teams.

---

## Core Truth

Good decks don't guarantee a win. Bad decks guarantee a loss.

Judges don't wake up wanting to watch pitch decks. They go through submissions because they have to. The moment they see something off, you lose their attention. A good deck just extends attention on your idea and sends you to the next round.

---

## Step 1: The One-Liner (5 words or less)

Before you open any slide tool, compress your startup into five words. If you can't, the idea lacks focus or you haven't sat with it long enough.

### Quality Hierarchy (in this order)

1. **Explicit** — what you're actually doing, explained clearly, without abstractions
2. **Non-ambiguous** — no room for misinterpretation
3. **Exciting** — makes someone want to know more
4. **Truthful** — paints the right picture (doesn't need 100% accuracy)

### Format: "X for Y"

Map your startup around a product people already know.

| Example | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Tokenized dinosaur fossils" | ✅ Good | Unambiguous, exciting, truthful |
| "Co-pilot for e-commerce" | ❌ Bad | Too abstract — shopper or shop owner? |
| "Reddit for agents" | ✅ Good | Clear analogy, timely |

### Test

Show your one-liner to someone outside your team. If they can't explain what you do back to you in their own words, it's not explicit enough.

---

## Step 2: The Blurb (1-2 paragraphs)

Expand on your one-liner using Pixar storytelling structure. Every children's book uses this — your pitch should too.

### Pixar Story Formula

1. **Context** — set the scene, explain the environment your startup lives in
2. **Problem** — there's an issue in that environment
3. **Hero rises** — you're solving something
4. **Why you** — background, traction, deep understanding nobody else has

### Example: Robinhood

> 1. **Context:** Stock market is an important way for consumers to build wealth
> 2. **Problem:** Current brokerages have high fees and make it complex to buy stocks
> 3. **Hero rises:** We're building a mobile app that lets retail investors easily open an account and trade stocks without paying any commission
> 4. **Why you:** So low friction that we can open up investing to a whole new generation. User base growing at 10% a month.

If the blurb doesn't sell them, no slide deck will.

---

## Step 3: The Founder Video

Record **before** building slides. It forces clarity.

### Rules

- **1 minute max** — you and your co-founders in the same room
- **No edits, no cuts** — if you stutter, record again from the top
- **Use Loom** — camera on while showing screen
- **Full screen** — no messy desktops

### Energy Standard

Watch the DoorDash YC application video (S13). That's the bar. You need to be so excited about what you're building that it shows on camera. If you're recording at 2am sleep-deprived, reschedule.

### Red Flag

If you can't talk about what you're building for two minutes straight without stuttering, you don't know your own idea well enough yet. Go back to the one-liner.

---

## Step 4: Slide Construction

### Format Rules

- **Sharing:** Google Slides (links, not PDFs). Mentors need to comment directly.
- **Blurb:** Google Docs (link, not attachment)
- **Video:** Loom (link, not file)
- **Branding:** Black background, white font. No branded themes.
- **Length:** Max 300 words / 180 seconds across entire deck
- **Titles:** Never use "The Problem", "The Solution" — write actual headlines

### What Each Slide Should Do

Every slide has one job: make the judge want to see the next slide. If a slide doesn't create forward momentum, cut it.

---

## The Don'ts (Complete List)

| Category | Rule |
|---|---|
| Problem | Don't specify the problem as a lack of solution. "My city doesn't have parking" = meaningless. "People want to park within a 5-minute walk from home" = real desire, real willingness to pay. |
| Data | Don't make up numbers. Cite your sources. |
| Length | Don't exceed 300 words or 180 seconds. |
| Language | Don't use buzzwords: "future of", "revolutionizing", "disrupting", etc. |
| Media | Don't add fluff, music, videos, GIFs. Don't use animations (besides basic slide appearance). |
| Design | Don't brand your deck. Black background, white font. |
| Slides | Don't use "The Problem" / "The Solution" titles. Don't do text-heavy slides. |
| Video | Don't use headphones. Don't record without full screen + Loom. Don't edit the video. Don't mumble during pauses — embrace silence. |
| Process | Don't reuse your previous deck. Don't take advice from multiple mentors (pick one, iterate with them). Don't procrastinate. Don't have anyone pitch other than yourself. |
| Delivery | Don't use complex words. Don't forget to smile. |

## Pre-Flight Checklist

Before submitting, verify:

- [ ] One-liner is ≤5 words, passes the stranger test
- [ ] Blurb follows Pixar structure (context → problem → hero → why you)
- [ ] Founder video is ≤60s, uncut, full screen + camera, genuine energy
- [ ] Deck shared as Google Slides link (not PDF)
- [ ] Blurb shared as Google Doc link (not PDF)
- [ ] Video shared as Loom link (not file)
- [ ] No buzzwords anywhere
- [ ] No "The Problem" / "The Solution" slide titles
- [ ] All numbers cited
- [ ] Black background, white font, no branding
- [ ] Total deck ≤300 words
- [ ] You smiled

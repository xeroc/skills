## What a Pitch deck should look like

Angel Investor-Specific Tips

### Angel investors care more about

- Founder quality
- Vision
- Early validation
- Speed of execution

### Less about

- Perfect financial models
- Complex market sizing
- Detailed corporate structure

### Common Mistakes

❌ Too much text per slide
❌ Technical jargon
❌ No customer validation
❌ Unrealistic projections
❌ 30+ slide decks
❌ Spending 10 slides on the product and 1 slide on traction

### What Gets Attention

A great opening formula:

Problem → Why now → Solution → Proof → Big opportunity

Example:

"European SMEs spend €10B annually on compliance reporting. New regulations are increasing the burden. Our AI platform cuts reporting effort by 95%. We already have 12 paying customers and €8k MRR. We're raising €500k to scale sales."

That sequence quickly answers the questions most angels have in the first few minutes.

## What an investor wants to see

For an angel investor, **time is scarce, risk is high, and the emotional connection is critical.** We see hundreds of pitches a year. We are not looking for a "perfect" PowerPoint; we are looking for a **trustworthy narrative** that proves you can turn our money into 10x–100x returns.

Here is the blueprint for what a pitch **must have**, **must do**, and **must look like** to pass the "sniff test."

### 1. What it MUST HAVE (The Non-Negotiable Content)

Angels invest in a specific order: **Team > Market > Product.** If the team slides fail, we stop listening.

- **The "Why You" Slide (Founder-Market Fit):** Do not list your resume. List your _scar tissue_. What specific, painful problem did you experience in this industry that no one else saw? Why are you and your co-founder the only people on earth who can survive the next 5 years of hell to build this?
- **The "Moat" (Not just "First Mover"):** We don't care if you are "first." We care if you are **defensible**. Show us network effects, proprietary data, a unique manufacturing process, or a regulatory advantage. If a giant like Google or a copycat with $5M can do this in 6 months, we are out.
- **The "Traction Trifecta":** If you are pre-revenue, show us _qualitative_ traction (LOIs, waiting lists, pilot agreements). If you are post-revenue, show us **retention** and **unit economics** (CAC vs. LTV). Top-line revenue is vanity; gross margin and retention are sanity.
- **The "Use of Funds" (Line-by-Line):** Do not say "30% Marketing, 40% R&D." Give us a clear, detailed table: _"Hire Senior React Dev ($120k), Facebook Ad Testing ($15k), Legal Patent Filing ($10k)."_ We need to see you know exactly where every dollar goes for the next 18 months.

---

### 2. What it MUST DO (The Emotional & Strategic Effect)

A pitch is a sales conversation, not a data dump. It must accomplish three psychological goals.

- **Kill the "So What?" Question:** Every feature you list must be immediately followed by the benefit. _Wrong: "We use AI." Right: "We use AI to cut manual data entry from 4 hours to 4 minutes, saving our clients $40k/year."_
- **Create "FOMO" (Fear Of Missing Out):** You must subtly show that this round is _competitive_ without being arrogant. Mention that you are "closing the round in 60 days" and that "two strategic angels are already in." Angels follow other smart angels. If no one else has written a check, we assume you are a lemon.
- **Show "The Pivot" (Intellectual Honesty):** Angels know your initial plan will fail. We want to see that you are _listening to the data_. Dedicate 30 seconds to saying: _"We originally thought X, but customer feedback taught us Y, so we pivoted to Z. That pivot doubled our retention."_ This proves you are coachable—which is more important than your initial idea.

---

### 3. What it MUST LOOK LIKE (The Aesthetics & Delivery)

Do not confuse "polished" with "corporate." You are a startup, not a Fortune 500 company.

- **The 10/20/30 Rule (Guy Kawasaki):** **10 slides.** **20 minutes** (even if you have an hour). **30-point font** (minimum). If I have to squint to read your financials, I will assume you are hiding something.
- **Visuals over Text:** Use high-quality mockups, screenshots of your actual dashboard, and photos of your users using the product. _Stocksy_ photos of "happy teams" make you look generic. Screenshots of a messy Excel sheet that your software automates makes you look real.
- **The "One-Liner" on the Title Slide:** At the very top of your opening slide, in giant text, put your **"Headline."** Example: _"Uber for Industrial HVAC Repair"_ or _"Shopify for Dental Practices."_ If I can't explain your business to my spouse in 5 seconds, I can't invest.
- **The "Appendix" (The Secret Weapon):** Keep your main deck clean. Put all the dense data (market sizing charts, technical architecture, full financial projections) in an **Appendix** at the back. When I ask a tough question about your churn rate, you flip to the appendix and show me the graph. That makes you look like a pro who has thought of everything.

---

### The Golden Rule for Angels

**Do not pitch the _opportunity_; pitch the _risk mitigation_.** We already know the market is big. We don't need you to convince us of the dream. We need you to convince us that **you are the lowest-risk bet** in a high-risk casino.

**One final, harsh piece of advice:** If you use the word "Unprecedented" or "Disruptive" in your first 3 minutes, we are mentally checking out. Speak like a human, know your numbers cold, and make eye contact with the person who hasn't spoken yet (they are usually the smartest one in the room).

## Output

A single-page investor website gives you an advantage over a traditional deck because investors can **scroll through a narrative** rather than click through disconnected slides.

The best-performing structure is often not:

> Problem → Solution → Market → Team

but rather:

> Future → Why now → Evidence → Business → Ask

This feels more like a founder telling a story and less like a corporate presentation.

Here's a markdown template designed specifically for a **single-page investor site**.

# [Startup Name]

## The Future We Are Building

Imagine a world where...

In that world:

- Outcome 1
- Outcome 2
- Outcome 3

Today, that future is becoming possible because of:

- Market shift
- Technology shift
- Regulatory shift
- Behavioral shift

We are building the company that makes it happen.

A few things make this format feel more premium than a deck:

- Start with the **future vision**, not the problem.
- Put **traction/proof above market size**.
- Introduce a **contrarian insight** ("everyone thinks X, we believe Y").
- End with a strong vision section before the fundraising ask.
- Use full-width screenshots, diagrams, customer logos, and metrics between sections when you convert the markdown into a website.
